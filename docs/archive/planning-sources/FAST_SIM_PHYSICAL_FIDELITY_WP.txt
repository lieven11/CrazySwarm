## Outcome

Fast Sim remains deterministic and fast while gaining the minimum physical coupling
needed to reason about flight performance: battery state and terminal voltage affect
available motor voltage, current, thrust, saturation, and endurance; rotor forces use
the physical vehicle geometry; payload and center of mass affect dynamics; modeled
sensor errors affect the estimator/control path; and bounded aerodynamic corrections
cover the low-speed indoor operating envelope.

The result is still a reduced-order engineering simulator. It must not be described
as hardware-qualified, a digital twin, blade-resolved aerodynamics, or a replacement
for measured bench and contained-flight evidence.

## Fast Sim versus Isaac decision

The implementation in this packet belongs in Fast Sim:

- battery open-circuit voltage, internal resistance, discharge and recovery;
- averaged electrical current and power limits;
- firmware-style battery thrust compensation and PWM saturation;
- voltage-dependent motor/propeller thrust and actuator response;
- exact rotor positions, reaction torque, mass, payload, center of mass and inertia;
- per-motor variation and failure/degradation overlays;
- IMU, Flow, range and estimator error models appropriate to fast simulation;
- body-axis drag and a reduced-order ground-effect model; and
- deterministic qualification matrices for the above.

There is no current Isaac task to add to `ACTIVE_SOFTWARE_WP.md`. A future live Isaac
worker should consume the same versioned vehicle/powertrain parameter set and apply
the same rotor forces to its rigid body. Isaac remains the later target for resolved
body contact, friction, bounce, tumble, detailed collision geometry, complex raycast
occlusion, and rendered camera/depth sensors. Isaac does not supply Crazyflie battery,
motor, propeller, or wake physics automatically.

Blade-resolved propeller flow, CFD-quality wall/ceiling interaction, detailed swarm
wakes, crash deformation, and thermal damage are not silently assigned to either
backend by this packet. They require a separate future decision and evidence plan.

## Historical model-v1 baseline and reason physical fidelity was reopened

The preserved model-v1 baseline was internally stable but had the following verified
limitations. Model v2 corrects these software-structure issues without relabeling the
historical v1 evidence:

- Motor target thrust depends on normalized command and configured maximum thrust,
  not on battery voltage.
- Battery voltage is calculated after motor thrust/current and affects only telemetry
  and hard cutoff.
- A forced 5% takeoff and 10-second hover produces the same altitude, thrust demand,
  peak current, and motor command as a 100% run.
- A direct 120-second hover starting at 5% can reach 0% state of charge and continue
  flying because the modeled hover voltage remains slightly above cutoff.
- The normal supervisor correctly masks part of this plant defect by rejecting
  takeoff below 30% and requesting recovery at the critical threshold.
- Full command produces an abrupt voltage-cutoff cliff instead of progressively
  reduced thrust headroom.
- The current motor mixer is a plus layout; the Crazyflie firmware force/torque
  distribution represents an X layout.
- `center_of_mass_body_m` is declared but does not enter the force or torque equations.
- Payload changes total mass but does not update combined center of mass or inertia.
- The IMU output is exact physics specific force and angular rate. The accumulated
  error currently called drift is injected into horizontal position/Flow estimation,
  not into an IMU bias/noise process.
- Current variation is a consequence of controller command variation only; PWM ripple,
  commutation, electrical measurement noise, and motor vibration are not modeled.
- Aerodynamics are limited to one isotropic linear-drag coefficient; ground effect,
  body-axis quadratic drag, rotor gyroscopic effects, and propeller variation are absent.

Focused software verification at review time passed 47 physics, qualification, and
estimator tests. Those tests establish software consistency, not physical accuracy.

## Physical-claim and provenance rules

No coefficient may be invented merely to make a trajectory look realistic.

1. Exact-aircraft controlled measurements with uncertainty are the preferred source.
2. Official Bitcraze data for the exact airframe, battery, motor, propeller and firmware
   configuration may seed a `CONFIGURED_UNQUALIFIED` model with recorded URL/version.
3. Data for an older or different Crazyflie configuration must remain explicitly
   identified as a provisional reference and may not be relabeled as the selected unit.
4. Generic quadrotor values, untraceable forum values, and visually tuned coefficients
   are not acceptable physical evidence.
5. Every physical parameter records value, unit, source, source version/date, applicable
   hardware configuration, qualification class, and uncertainty when available.
6. Until bench evidence exists, all executable physical coefficients remain
   `CONFIGURED_UNQUALIFIED`, even if their structure is physically motivated.
7. `PHYSICALLY_MEASURED` is permitted only after the existing Reality hardware procedure
   produces immutable reviewed evidence for the exact aircraft/configuration.
8. Software tests may verify equations, invariants and deterministic behavior; they may
   not promote a parameter or outcome to physical truth.

## Compatibility and model-version policy

These changes intentionally alter canonical dynamics. They must not be hidden under
`crazyflie-6dof@1.0.0`.

- Introduce a new physics model version, provisionally `crazyflie-6dof@2.0.0`.
- Preserve model-v1 receipts, replay identity, and the ability to identify old runs.
- Do not silently reinterpret old scenario hashes with new powertrain semantics.
- Add an explicit migration for legacy battery drain fields or reject ambiguous input.
- Keep simulator adapter contract `1.0.0` unless command, frame, unit, state, or required
  signal meaning actually changes.
- Regenerate canonical outcome hashes only after old/new comparisons and an intentional
  model-version review.

## FAST-SIM-PHYS-WP-01 — Couple battery, electrical power and motor thrust

**Priority:** P0 critical
**Status:** Software complete
**Depends on:** FAST-SIM-PHYS-WP-00

### Outcome

Battery state and load determine terminal voltage; terminal voltage and compensated PWM
determine motor voltage, current, thrust and saturation; zero available energy cannot
produce sustained positive thrust.

### Tasks

- [x] Replace the linear full-to-empty voltage interpolation with a configurable
      open-circuit-voltage-versus-state-of-charge curve or table.
- [x] Retain Coulomb counting but define capacity at the modeled condition and prevent
      state-of-charge clamping from creating unlimited residual energy.
- [x] Solve terminal voltage and total current consistently, including electronics/deck
      parasitic load and connector/internal resistance.
- [x] Add a bounded load-line solution for `V_terminal = OCV - I_total * R_effective`;
      non-convergence must fail explicitly, never reuse a plausible-looking stale value.
- [x] Add firmware-style battery compensation that converts desired thrust/motor voltage
      to PWM using filtered terminal voltage.
- [x] Model the measured nonlinear motor-voltage-to-thrust relation with a versioned
      polynomial or lookup table and explicit valid range.
- [x] Model averaged motor current consistently with motor voltage/thrust. Do not resolve
      high-frequency PWM ripple at a 100 Hz physics step; use a documented averaged model.
- [x] Apply per-motor PWM, motor-current and thrust limits plus a battery total-current or
      power limit appropriate to the configured cell.
- [x] Expose requested versus available thrust and motor saturation in model telemetry.
- [x] Make maximum available thrust non-increasing as terminal voltage falls for an
      otherwise identical hardware state.
- [x] Add undervoltage persistence/hysteresis or recovery semantics so a single numerical
      crossing is not confused with a fully modeled cutoff/recovery cycle.
- [x] Guarantee that zero state of charge, invalid cell state, or an authoritative cutoff
      cannot sustain hover thrust.
- [x] Add optional condition inputs for capacity scale, resistance scale, battery age and
      temperature; keep them neutral and unqualified until evidence exists.
- [x] Preserve the supervisor's 30% takeoff gate and critical-battery recovery behavior;
      the plant correction must not weaken policy.
- [x] Remove or explicitly migrate legacy percentage-per-second drain behavior so it does
      not double count energy with the electrical model.

### Verification matrix

- State of charge: 100%, 50%, 30%, 20%, 10%, 5% and 0%.
- Load: idle, steady hover, vertical acceleration, maximum collective and asymmetric
  attitude correction.
- Battery condition: nominal, increased resistance, reduced capacity and configured
  temperature/age scale.
- Controller mode: compensation enabled, compensation disabled, saturation and cutoff.
- Fixed step: 0.005 s, 0.010 s and 0.020 s.

### Required invariants

- Electrical energy does not increase without an explicit charging source.
- Applied motor power and thrust never exceed configured voltage/current/PWM limits.
- A lower terminal-voltage ceiling cannot provide more maximum thrust than a higher one.
- Compensation may preserve hover thrust only while sufficient PWM/current headroom exists.
- At low voltage, required PWM rises before saturation; after saturation, thrust shortfall
  is visible to the controller and telemetry.
- State of charge at zero cannot remain airborne indefinitely.
- Cutoff state, reason and recovery behavior are deterministic and evidence-visible.

### Acceptance

- The old diagnostic in which 5% and 100% have identical complete performance no longer
  passes. Equal hover thrust is allowed only when battery compensation has headroom, and
  then PWM/headroom/power state must still differ physically.
- Forced 5% operation either retains only measured/calibrated limited authority or reaches
  saturation/cutoff; it never receives unbounded nominal authority.
- The normal supervised 5% preflight remains rejected.
- Endurance and maximum-thrust claims remain `CONFIGURED_UNQUALIFIED` until bench data exists.

not mislabeled truth or directly injected position drift, while Fast Sim retains a clearly

### Tasks

- [x] Add independent sensor sample clocks, latency buffers and held-sample semantics;
      sensor rate must not be silently identical to physics rate.
- [x] Add per-axis IMU white noise, constant bias, bias random walk, scale error,
      misalignment, bandwidth/filtering, clipping and timestamp latency.
- [x] Keep IMU values exact when all configured error coefficients are zero.

      official datasheet baseline; do not tune them to create attractive drift.
- [x] Separate physical IMU errors from Flow/position-estimator drift in telemetry,

- [ ] Model accelerometer vibration as a bounded sampled/filtered error or aggregate

- [x] Model Flow observation error as a function of height, tilt, motion, surface/lighting

- [x] Ensure Flow output is an observation consumed by the estimator path, not merely

- Allan-deviation/noise-density comparison when physical IMU logs become available.
- Bias, random walk, clipping, latency and dropout each change estimator/control outcomes
